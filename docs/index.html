    About

    Data

    Download counts come from ClickPy, ClickHouse's public copy of the PyPI download logs, which goes back to 2016; the logs are PyPI's download statistics, licensed CC BY 4.0, and are shown here aggregated. "All" leaves out installers that mirror PyPI wholesale (bandersnatch, devpi, Nexus, Artifactory, z3c.pypimirror). The period of 30 to 180 days shows the 7-day rolling mean of daily downloads; "All years" shows the mean per day of each month. The data are refreshed monthly.

    Packages

    Listed packages are a hand-picked list, every PyPI package of best-of-atomistic-machine-learning, and the simulation engines of atomistic.software that install from PyPI. The page also shows the most-downloaded dependents: packages whose latest release requires a listed package, directly or through other dependents, named in the "Found via" column; a dependency declared only in an optional extra does not count, and general-purpose libraries such as RDKit and DGL are not followed. Each dependent is put by hand in the category of what it does; one not yet reviewed takes the category of the package it was found through. Suggest a package.

    Downloads are not users

    CI services, container builds and cluster jobs install packages over and over, so download counts measure installs, not people. Two columns of the table estimate this over the last days. CI is the share whose installer reported a CI run; pip and uv report one when a variable such as CI is set, as on GitHub Actions and GitLab CI. Bulk rebuilds is the share that came, without a CI flag, from one pinned environment downloading the same version at least times on one day (same installer, Python, OS, glibc and country): a Docker image or batch job rebuilt many times rather than many users.

    Automated installs run almost entirely on Linux, so "macOS + Windows" leaves them out; it also leaves out real use on Linux workstations and clusters, and is therefore a floor on interactive use, not an estimate of it. Installs from outside PyPI, such as conda-forge, are not counted.

    Using the figures

    The figures and the numbers behind them are free to reuse, also commercially, in papers, slides and web pages, under CC BY 4.0: credit "Atomistic PyPI usage" with a link, as in How to cite.

    Each chart downloads as PNG or SVG, drawn in light colours with its title, the filters in use and the data source, ready for a slide; CSV gives the numbers behind it. The bar chart race records as a 1280x720 video (MP4 or WebM, depending on the browser) in real time; keep the tab in view while it records. Share gives a link to one figure alone and the code to embed it in another web page; both keep the filters in use and follow the monthly refresh, so cite a download rather than an embed where the numbers must not change. The address bar keeps the current view, so a link reproduces it.

    PyPI as a whole has grown too, and much of that growth is automated installs. The baseline of all of PyPI (ClickPy's monthly totals over every project, all platforms, mirrors included) shows whether the field grew faster than that: see "Per million PyPI downloads" in The field over time and the dashed line in Rising and fading. The package list is today's, so packages that were popular once and are no longer listed are missing from the early years.
